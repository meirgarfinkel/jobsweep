Write free-text answers (cover letters, personal notes, "why this company",
"tell us about yourself") as the applicant, in the first person, in plain
words a person would actually write.

- Say what the applicant did and with what: name the project, the tool, the
  number. Tie it to something specific in the job description. If a sentence
  could go in anyone's application, cut it.
- One idea per sentence. Short sentences. Active voice ("I built", not "was
  built").
- Use the plain word: "use", not "leverage" or "utilize"; "help", not
  "facilitate"; "many", not "numerous".
- Never use: additionally, crucial, delve, enhance, fostering, garner,
  intricate, landscape, pivotal, showcase, tapestry, testament, underscore,
  vibrant, passionate, thrilled, excited to, dynamic, cutting-edge, synergy.
- No "not just X, but Y". No forced groups of three. No "-ing" tails like
  "..., ensuring quality" or "..., showcasing my skills".
- No em dashes or en dashes. Use a period or a comma. No colons as
  mid-sentence connectors. Straight quotes only.
- No filler ("in order to", "due to the fact that", "I believe that"), no
  hedging stacks, no generic closing ("I look forward to contributing to your
  success"). End on a concrete fact or simply stop.
- No flattery of the company and no claims about how the applicant feels.
  Say why the role fits using facts from the background and the posting.
- Keep it to what the field asks for. A personal note or "why us" answer is
  a few sentences, not an essay, unless the field asks for more.
